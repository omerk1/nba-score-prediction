## Area 1 — Information lost to aggregation

**Measured, not assumed: the headline finding is a null result.** Built the exact point-in-time construction (`shift(1)` + `groupby(team).rolling(w)`) for candidate rolling-std and trend statistics of scoring margin and offensive output, on the full 11,969-game history (11,662 after requiring full L20 history both teams), and tested two things directly:

**1. Does rolling std add information the existing rolling mean doesn't (for the point estimate)?** Regressed `POINT_DIFF` on the 6 mean columns already in the model (`home/away_diff_avg_L{5,10,20}`), took the residual, correlated it against 6 candidate std columns:

| candidate | corr with residual |
|---|---:|
| home/away `diff_std_L5/L10/L20` | −0.025 to +0.026 (all 6) |

Adding all 6 std columns to the baseline regression moves R² from 0.1462 → 0.1475 (Δ+0.0013 — under 1% relative). Same test on `TOTAL_POINTS`/`off_std`: R² 0.1534 → 0.1542 (Δ+0.0008). **This is a genuinely weak result, not a measurement artifact** — n=11,662 gives real statistical power; the correlations are small in *practical* magnitude, not just noisy.

**2. Does std at least predict the *size* of the prediction error (heteroskedasticity — the calibration-relevant version of "consistency vs. level," and closer to what the distributional/betting framing actually needs)?** Correlated `|residual|` against the std columns directly: point-diff side tops out at **+0.024** (`away_diff_std_L20`), total-points side is **effectively zero, one sign even negative** (`away_off_std_L20`: −0.0099). No support here either.

**Conclusion: the "aggregation discards volatility, and volatility is predictive" hypothesis does not hold up under direct measurement for point-diff or total-points, linearly, in either the point-estimate or the heteroskedasticity framing.** This doesn't prove a tree model could extract zero value from std under interaction effects a linear probe can't see — but there's no positive evidence for it here, and the honest prior for this project (a repeated pattern: `season_motivation`, `on_off_splits`, KNN-style-matchup all looked plausible and returned small/negative CV results) should not default to "try it anyway." **Not recommended as a priority action.** If revisited, it should be a single, narrowly-scoped full-CV test (not the "extend to all families" version) explicitly framed as *disconfirming a linear prior*, not as an expected win.

**3. Trend (L5−L20 momentum) is structurally, not just empirically, redundant.** `trend = diff_avg_L5 − diff_avg_L20` is an exact linear function of two columns already in the model (`r`(trend, L5)=0.698, `r`(trend, L20)≈0.00 — mechanically expected since L5 dominates the difference). Regressing on the full L5/L10/L20 baseline and correlating the residual against trend gives **r = 0.0000** — not "small," exactly zero, because a linear model's residual is orthogonal by construction to any linear combination of its own inputs. CatBoost's nonlinear splits *could* still benefit from being handed the combination pre-computed (saves the tree from spending splits to reconstruct it), but that's a materially weaker, harder-to-evidence case than std's. **Drop trend from consideration entirely — it's not "weak," it's definitionally redundant given L5 and L20 already exist as separate columns.**

**One partial exception already in the codebase, unaffected by the above**: `style_fingerprint_features` (rank #1, 27.4% share) does NOT use a flat mean — `fingerprint.py`'s exponential decay-weighting (`halflife=13.2` games) is proven, adopted infrastructure. The null result above is about *adding new statistics* (std, trend) to families that currently only compute means; it says nothing against decay-weighting itself, which already earns its place. Nobody has tested applying decay-weighting to `rolling_features`/`style_features` specifically (as opposed to `style_fingerprint_features`, where it already lives) — that's a distinct, still-open question from the std/trend one, and lower-effort to test than either (swap an existing, already-tested function in, no new columns at all — pure replace, zero parsimony cost). Flagging as the one aggregation-related idea that survives this round's skepticism, precisely because it doesn't add columns.

**Verdict for Area 1**: current mean-based aggregation for `rolling_features`/`style_features`/`opponent_quality_features`/`home_advantage_features` is, on the evidence gathered, **adequate** — the specific "add dispersion/trend statistics" fix is not supported. The one live idea is a decay-weighting *swap* (replace, not add) for `rolling_features`/`style_features`, motivated by precedent rather than new measurement, and cheap enough to test on that basis alone.

## Area 2 — Construction audit

### Elo (`src/feature_engineering/elo.py`) — answered point by point

- **Margin of victory**: yes, and correctly scaled. `mult = log(margin+1) * (2.2 / (elo_diff_winner*0.001 + 2.2))` (`elo.py:74-77`) — standard 538 formula. The log term means a 60-point win doesn't move the rating 6× more than a 10-point win (log(61)/log(11) ≈ 1.7×, not 6×) — bounded, not exploitable by farming blowouts. The second term additionally *dampens* credit when the winner was already heavily favored, so beating an already-weak team repeatedly doesn't compound. **Sound, no issue.**
- **Season boundaries**: regression-to-mean at season transition, `ratings[team] = initial + (1 - season_regression) * (ratings[team] - initial)` (`elo.py:63`), `season_regression=0.522`. **Already re-verified under full 5-fold CV** (E1, session `rs_20260808_1`): grid over {0.30, 0.40, 0.522, 0.65, 0.80}, essentially flat (val_mean range 1.3841–1.3852), no candidate cleared the fold2-5 guardrail. This axis is closed, not open — don't re-run it.
- **Home advantage — is it double-counted?** Traced precisely: `compute_elo_ratings` uses `home_advantage` **only inside the expected-score formula** (`expected_home = 1/(1+10^(-(r_home+home_advantage-r_away)/400))`, `elo.py:71`) to correctly discount a home win that was already expected — this affects the *rating update delta*, but is never added to the persisted `ratings` dict itself, so the returned `home_team_elo`/`away_team_elo` are raw. Separately, `_add_elo_features` (`feature_builder.py:679-681`) constructs the actual `elo_diff` **feature** as `home_team_elo + home_advantage - away_team_elo` — home_advantage applied exactly once here. Grepped every `home_advantage` usage repo-wide (`config_loader.py`, `elo.py`, `feature_builder.py`, `season_motivation.py`'s own separate internal elo_diff calc) — no second application anywhere that reaches the model's actual inputs or predictions. **Verdict: not double-counted.** Two legitimate, distinct uses (rating-update fairness vs. predictive-feature construction), not the same effect applied twice. **This resolves the question raised — no fix needed.**
- **K-factor / home_advantage tuning provenance**: both (`k_factor=11.02`, `home_advantage=117.87`) were Optuna-tuned once via `tune_elo.py`, **pre-CV-harness**, and never independently re-verified under the CV protocol — E1 only swept `season_regression`. This is a real gap (inherited values, not CV-blessed), but E1's finding that the whole Elo config is fairly flat around current values (a 0.001-range spread across a wide `season_regression` grid) is at least suggestive the other two axes won't move much either — an extrapolation, not proof. **Legitimate but lower-priority open item — not in this memo's shortlist**, given the discipline against exhaustive tuning and the flat-result precedent already in hand.
- **Split Elo (offense/defense or pace-adjusted)? Evidence-based answer: no.** `docs/features/feature_eda_insights.md`'s existing correlation work already answers this precisely: `pace_score` (in `style_fingerprint_features`, individually the #1/#2-importance feature) correlates **~0** with `elo_diff` (-0.06/+0.05) — Elo structurally cannot see pace, so a pace-adjusted Elo variant would be trying to inject a signal that's *already present, more directly, in a separately-validated feature*. Meanwhile `style_offensive_rating_diff` (also in `style_fingerprint_features`, top-15 importance) correlates **0.75** with `elo_diff` — an offense-specific Elo split would be substantially redundant with a feature that already earns its own importance. **Decisive answer: don't split Elo — the decomposition this would add already exists elsewhere in the feature set, in a more granular, already-adopted form.** Splitting would cost real engineering + a new leakage surface (two accumulating rating processes instead of one) for a benefit largely already captured.

### Rest (`_add_rest_features`) — answered point by point, now with measurement

- **B2B vs. raw days-off**: yes, `back_to_back` (rest_days==1) exists as its own column alongside raw `rest_days` (`feature_builder.py:190`) — the sharpest rest effect is already isolated, not buried in a continuous count.
- **Home/away differential — measured, real, currently absent.** No differential exists anywhere in the codebase (confirmed by grep) — every other comparable family (`matchup_features`, `_compute_venue_delta`, injury's `team_deficit_diff`) builds one; rest doesn't. Built the differential myself and tested it against `POINT_DIFF` (n=11,969): raw per-team `rest_days`/`back_to_back` alone carry almost nothing (`corr(home_rest_days, POINT_DIFF)=-0.003`, `home_back_to_back` alone=-0.045, `away_back_to_back` alone=+0.059) — consistent with the family's current bottom-tier rank (11/13, permutation Δ0.0007). But the **constructed differential does meaningfully better than either raw column alone**: `b2b_diff` (home_b2b − away_b2b) correlates **−0.079** with `POINT_DIFF`, sign-sensible (home team uniquely on a back-to-back → real point-diff penalty), and `rest_diff` clipped to ±5 days (to control the All-Star-break outlier) correlates **+0.050**, roughly monotonic across a bucketed table (mean POINT_DIFF −2 days ≈ +0.48 → +2 days ≈ +4.74). **This is real, evidence-backed, and roughly 2-3× larger in magnitude than anything Area 1's std test found** — modest, on the same order as the family's current low importance, not a ceiling-breaker, but the strongest-evidenced concrete construction fix in this memo.
- **Multi-game fatigue density windows**: only one exists — `games_in_4_nights` (rolling 3-game date-gap sum ≤4, `feature_builder.py:191-193`). Confirmed via grep: no `4_in_6` or equivalent second density window. A second window is a plausible future add but has zero measurement behind it in this pass — not prioritized over the differential, which does.
- **Travel-adjusted / redundancy with travel features**: qualitative only (not numerically replicated this round — would need the team-city lookup table). Conceptually the two aren't redundant: `rest_days` is calendar-gap-based, `travel_miles` is geography-based, and they diverge concretely (a home-to-home back-to-back has `travel_miles≈0` but `back_to_back=1`). Treat this as an assumption carried forward, not a measured claim, if it matters for a future decision.

### Other top families — briefer, same lens

- **`opponent_quality_features` — construction explains the weakness concretely, this isn't a bug.** `_add_opponent_quality_features` (`feature_builder.py:228-273`) builds `opp_def_quality`/`opp_off_quality` by re-averaging the **exact same** `off_eff_L{w}`/`def_eff_L{w}` columns already live in `style_features`, just re-keyed over "opponents faced" instead of "own games" — a second-order aggregation of a primitive the model already sees directly. Schedule strength is a real, conceptually distinct axis from own-form, but in a roughly-balanced 82-game NBA schedule the cross-team variance in *opponent* quality is inherently much smaller than the variance in *own* quality — the signal ceiling here is structurally capped by the schedule's own balance, not a construction flaw. This is the concrete mechanism behind docs/EXPERIMENTS.md's "used but not predictive" framing (4th by CatBoost share, 6th by permutation). **No fix proposed — this is a case where the honest finding is "built correctly, inherently limited," not "broken."**
- **`_add_matchup_features`**: clean. All four sub-features are direct home-minus-away differentials of already-validated, leak-safe rolling columns. Nothing to flag.
- **`_add_home_advantage_features` (`_compute_venue_delta`) vs. Elo's `home_advantage` constant — confirmed complementary, checked numerically, not just conceptually.** `home_team_venue_delta_L20` (from `data/features/train_features.csv`, n=7,059): mean ≈2.04, std ≈4.0, range **−12 to +15 points** — real, per-team, time-varying spread. Elo's `home_advantage` is a single fixed 117.87-rating-point constant applied identically to all 30 teams. Not the same phenomenon at two granularities in a redundant way — one is a fixed league-wide prior baked into a rating system, the other an empirical, rolling, team-specific measurement. **No overlap concern.**
- **Cyclical/day-of-week**: confirmed absent in every form, not just unencoded — grep for `dayofweek`/`day_of_week`/`weekday` across `feature_builder.py` and `configs/config.yaml` returns zero hits. `season_progress` (a linear 0→1 ramp, not cyclical — doesn't wrap) is the only calendar-position feature that exists. Day-of-week specifically has no representation in any form. Real gap, but low expected value given `rest_days`/`back_to_back`/`games_in_4_nights` already cover the mechanism (schedule fatigue) a day-of-week feature would mostly proxy for indirectly. **Not prioritized.**

**Bugs found this round: none.** (The two bugs flagged in an earlier, less-careful pass of this memo — `h2h_win_pct_3yr`'s reindex issue and `injury_layer.py`'s multi-archetype accumulation — are both already fixed in the current code, per `docs/BACKLOG.md`'s A8 entry and `injury_layer.py`'s own docstring citing the prior D2 audit. Noted here once so neither gets re-chased.)

## Area 3 — Feature processing (CatBoost-specific, bounded-gain)

*(Scaling not investigated, per instruction — moot for trees.)*

- **Categorical handling — a genuine, argued (not measured) gap.** `ScorePredictor._create_model` never passes `cat_features` to `CatBoostRegressor`, and `configs/config.yaml`'s `features.exclude` list removes `HOME_TEAM_ID`/`AWAY_TEAM_ID` entirely (confirmed by direct read of the exclude list) — **team identity itself is not a model input in any form**, only aggregated stats about each team. CatBoost has no way to learn a team-specific fixed effect (arena quirks, any systematic bias not already captured by Elo/rolling stats) that isn't mediated through the engineered features. Declaring team ID as `cat_features` costs one line in `_create_model` + threading it through `cv_harness.run_split`. **This is the one Area 3 item with no direct measurement behind it this round (unlike rest, unlike the aggregation null result) — flagging that honestly.** It's argued from what's structurally absent, not evidenced from a correlation test; worth a cheap, isolated, full-CV test on its own merits (near-zero cost either way), but shouldn't be oversold as "measured."
- **Cyclical encoding**: covered under Area 2 above (day-of-week absent entirely) — not repeated here.
- **Missing-data representation**: not one uniform convention, but a real (if undocumented) split by feature *kind*, not ad hoc chaos. Rolling/style/opponent-quality/matchup/h2h families leave genuine NaN for early-career games (CatBoost handles natively). `_add_travel_features` and several `season_motivation.py` sub-scores `fillna(0.0)` when the underlying condition legitimately doesn't apply (no prior game to travel from ⇒ 0 miles is actually true, not "unknown"). `injury_features`' `zero_fill` vs `native_nan` choice **was already directly A/B tested** (E4, full 5-fold): `native_nan` lost specifically on the folds where injury data exists (1.3656 vs 1.3672 on folds 3-5). Given that one CV-confirmed data point already favors zero-fill-for-deltas, re-testing the same pattern on travel/season_motivation without a new angle is low expected value. **Not prioritized.**
- **Interaction features**: `_add_matchup_features` and `_compute_venue_delta` already hand-engineer real differentials. The one confirmed, evidence-backed gap is the rest differential (Area 2) — that's the actionable interaction-feature finding this round, not a new item here. Beyond that, hand-engineering further pairwise interactions (elo×rest, injury×style) is speculative — CatBoost's depth-6 trees can already combine two features within one tree, so the case for any of these is weaker than the rest-differential case, which mirrors a pattern already measured to work in this exact dataset (matchup_features' own adoption, plus this round's own b2b_diff measurement). **Not prioritized beyond the rest differential already covered.**
- **Leakage spot-check beyond D2**: `cv_harness.run_split` calls `feature_builder.create_all_features` three separate times (train/val/test), each bounded by its own `context_end_date` — confirmed by direct read (`cv_harness.py:186-223`). `_add_elo_features` and `_add_season_motivation_features` both explicitly respect `context_end_date`; every other rolling family routes through `.shift(1)` before any `.rolling()`/`.groupby().transform()` — checked `_add_opponent_quality_features`, `_add_home_advantage_features`, `_add_h2h_features` directly, no raw `.mean()`/`.std()` over an unsplit frame found anywhere. **No new leakage found** — clean structural re-confirmation beyond D2's fingerprint-specific audit.

## Area 4 — Modeling frame (assess only, not re-litigated)

Hierarchical/player-level modeling and raw-sequence neural nets: **no new evidence surfaced this round that changes either prior conclusion** — not reopened, per instruction.

- **Distributional objective (predict a distribution, not a point) — feasible, but explicitly metric-gated, not runnable as-is.** Today's calibration is a single global residual std (`norm.cdf(diff_pred / np.std(diff_true - diff_pred))`, `score_predictor.py`) applied identically to every game — no per-game uncertainty. CatBoost's `MultiQuantile`/`Quantile` loss is available natively (no new dependency) and is already an unexecuted item on `docs/EXPERIMENTS.md`'s own §3.3 watch-list. **The coupling that blocks this, stated plainly**: `compute_composite_score` — the thing every experiment in this project is judged against — only rewards point-estimate MAE (`diff_mae`/`total_mae`). A distributional objective could leave those unchanged or slightly worse while genuinely improving calibration, and the current metric has no way to see or credit that. This is not a "run it and check val_score_mean" experiment — it needs a metric decision first (a secondary, explicitly-not-composite-score calibration gate) or it can't even be evaluated fairly under this project's own rules. **Flagged, not proposed as a runnable CV experiment** — the decision needed is yours, not a modeling one.
- **Cross-class ensembling — feasible, low priority, not runnable as scoped.** Only CatBoost is installed; a genuine second model class means either a new dependency or a same-library-different-bias fallback (e.g. a plain linear/ridge model on a curated subset, using the already-imported `sklearn`). Given family-importance shows this dataset's ceiling is presently more feature-driven than model-driven (Area 1/2's findings above), and an ensemble doubles training cost per future CV screen while adding a new blend-weight tunable (itself needing CV-not-static-set tuning, which the discipline explicitly warns against over-fitting), this is a real but lower-value use of the next research session's budget. **Flagged, not prioritized.**

## Decisive shortlist

**Test first (in priority order):**

1. **Rest/back-to-back differential** (`b2b_diff`, `rest_diff` clipped ±5 days) added to `_add_rest_features`, mirroring the differential pattern already proven in `_add_matchup_features`/`_compute_venue_delta`. **Why first**: this is the only Area 1/2 hypothesis in this memo with real, measured, sign-sensible, roughly-monotonic evidence behind it (`corr=-0.079` for b2b_diff — 2-3× the magnitude Area 1's std test found for a hypothesis I'd previously over-ranked). Cheap (2 new columns, arithmetic on already-existing data), replaces nothing but is small enough not to raise the parsimony bar much, full-CV-gated like every prior experiment.
2. **`style_features`/`opponent_quality_features` rolling-window trim** (drop L10, keep L5+L20 — E3's already-scoped, VIF-motivated proposal). Unaffected by this round's new measurement, still the cleanest pure-subtraction item available (net-negative column count, near-zero overfitting cost).
3. **Decay-weighting swap for `rolling_features`/`style_features`** (reuse `fingerprint.py`'s already-tuned `_decayed_weighted_mean`, replacing the flat mean rather than adding a new column). The one Area 1 idea that survives this round's skepticism specifically because it's a *replace*, not an *add* — motivated by precedent (already proven for `style_fingerprint_features`) rather than fresh measurement, so rank it behind the two items with direct evidence, but ahead of anything else in this memo on cost/parsimony grounds.

**Explicitly deprioritized / dropped, and why:**

- **Rolling std/variance and trend features** (last round's top pick) — measured directly this round, found not supported (all correlations <0.03, R² deltas <0.15% of baseline, trend mathematically redundant with existing columns). Dropping from the active list; not worth a CV slot on the evidence gathered. This is the header finding of this pass, not a footnote.
- **Elo modifications** (season_regression: already closed by E1; k_factor/home_advantage: real gap but unmeasured this round, and E1's flat-grid result makes a big payoff unlikely; offense/defense split: decisively argued against — the decomposition already exists elsewhere in the feature set, in a more granular and already-adopted form). None of these earn a CV slot ahead of items 1-3 above.
- **`opponent_quality_features` "fix"** — not attempted, because the construction audit explains *why* it's structurally capped (schedule balance limits the achievable signal), not that it's built wrong. Nothing to fix.
- **Team ID as `cat_features`** — legitimate and cheap, but genuinely unmeasured this round (an argued gap, not an evidenced one, unlike item 1). Worth a low-cost isolated test at some point, but doesn't clear the bar to be in the top 3 given items with real measurement behind them exist.
- **Distributional objective / ensembling** — both feasible but explicitly gated on decisions above this analysis's pay grade (a metric-definition call, and a scope/effort call respectively). Flagged for your judgment, not queued as experiments.
